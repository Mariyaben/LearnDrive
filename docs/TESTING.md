# Desk validation and device acceptance

## Current spatial integration

See [INTEGRATION.md](INTEGRATION.md) for the pinned Supercombo contract, current
artifact, completed checks and physical acceptance gaps. Run the native CMake
suite with `ctest --test-dir build/native-spatial --output-on-failure` and compare
the real-model run using
`python tools/verify_supercombo.py --native-prefix build/native-spatial/supercombo-smoke`.
The driving path is deterministic; language models are invoked only by an
explicit asynchronous post-drive summary request. The active Android spatial
model is Supercombo, with YOLO labels at 4 Hz; TwinLiteNet is retained as an
inactive legacy asset. The APK contains 43 recorded prompts.

Camera and tactical guidance share a 3.5-second non-emergency cooldown and
10-second duplicate lock. Active braking suppresses routine advice, but TTI
below 1.2 seconds forces Level 0 “Brake harder” and immediate audio preemption.
The older Desk simulation below exercises the separate legacy telemetry rules;
its blanket braking suppression and emergency cooldown are not the live
spatial-reflex contract.

Android analysis targets 30 Hz normally and 25/20 Hz at light/moderate thermal
levels; severe heat reduces it further and critical heat pauses analysis.
These scheduling limits do not prove delivered FPS. Run
`python tools/profile_android.py --seconds 1801` on a physical ARM64 device
to collect the actual duration, FPS, thermal status and decision-latency gates.
Physical audio timing, camera accuracy, thermal stability and iOS compilation
remain unverified in this Windows environment.

## Built-in test mode

1. Android opens in Camera mode. Tap **Test voice**, raise media volume and confirm audible playback. Point the camera at a person or vehicle and check the green detection boxes. Select **Desk** and wait for **Offline voice ready** to run the scenarios below. Use airplane mode throughout the session.
2. Enable **Manual vehicle · gear guidance**, set speed to 20 km/h, pitch to 6 degrees, and TTI to 20 seconds. After 1.5 seconds “Uphill. Lower gear if needed” should play once. Leave pitch unchanged: it must remain quiet. Hold pitch at zero for at least two seconds to re-arm; set pitch to −6 degrees for “Downhill. Use engine braking.” Calibration must be on level ground for real slope sensing.
3. Set speed to 65 km/h, pitch to zero and TTI to 2 seconds. After the 3.5 second gap, expect “Ease off accelerator.”
4. Enable simulated driver braking. Further warnings, including emergency warnings, must be suppressed.
5. Disable braking and set TTI to 0.8 seconds. After the gap, expect “Collision risk. Brake now.” At 20 km/h with TTI 2 seconds, expect “Brake gently.”
6. Load `scenarios/desk.csv`. At times 2.5, 4.5, 8.1 and 12 seconds the desktop rules harness emits steep-uphill, ease, emergency and brake prompts. At time 8 braking suppresses the warning. Mobile playback timing begins when the script is loaded; voice readiness may cause early rows to be skipped.
7. Select **Normalize phone plane · level ground · hold still 5 s**, point the rear camera forward with the phone in its intended mount, and hold still on level ground until normalized. Enable physical IMU input in Desk. Tilting should change pitch; a sharp tap should show shock and briefly inhibit warnings. A hand gesture cannot reproduce vehicle braking accurately; the braking slider supplies that case.
8. Select a local MP4 with both vision models installed. Telemetry stays simulated. Decoder failures, missing weights and incompatible tensors must be visible, and no invented hazard is emitted.
9. Use **Test audio preemption** to start the uphill prompt, then interrupt it with emergency PCM after 250 ms. Desk/telemetry-based prompts keep their strict 3.5-second cooldown, including emergency prompts. Android camera guidance can escalate urgency immediately: a close-obstacle caution interrupts routine speech, and a reliable imminent-collision warning interrupts the caution. Bend/gear/slope guidance can interrupt image-only cautions of equal priority. Reliable approach and collision prompts retain higher priority. Shared prompt policy controls playback rather than numeric clip IDs.
10. In **Camera**, leave **Camera pointed at a road video (test)** ON, deny location permission or disable Location, and set **Video / fallback driving speed** to the speed in the test video. The startup instruction must speak before physical calibration and before any road user appears. Show a person/vehicle: the first fresh detection gives scene guidance without finite TTI. Show a large central vehicle in a stationary test image/video: the image-only obstacle caution must speak without box expansion or a road-mask match, using gap/stop-preparation wording. TTI **not estimated** must not silence these messages. Side traffic and small boxes must produce routine guidance rather than a close-obstacle caution. At 3–5 km/h, routine obstacle/road-user speech must not say to slow down. A reliable imminent collision must still request braking while moving, including at a crawl; stationary guidance says to stay alert.
11. Turn video test OFF for GPS/manual selection checks. Leave **Require GPS speed** OFF and set **Video / fallback driving speed** to 30 km/h; the speed display identifies **manual fallback** when no fix is available. Enable **Require GPS speed** without a fix: only the speed-based path is blocked; basic camera guidance continues. Relaunch and check saved settings. Valid GPS, including zero speed, takes priority over manual speed only with video test OFF. Physical calibration/fresh IMU/braking/shock gates remain on the tactical path. Braking/shock suppress camera action cues in either mode. Check that an unchanged obstacle is announced once, two seconds of confirmed clear frames re-arm it, missing frames do not re-arm it, and escalating risk can produce an urgent cue without waiting for routine speech.

12. In Camera video-test mode, test left/right bend clips with the phone stationary. Bend guidance must work without GPS or physical pitch calibration, and remain quiet on a straight road. Check the yellow road trace and Road status. With manual gear guidance ON, the bend cue includes selecting speed/gear before the bend; with it OFF and low/unknown speed, it avoids an easing command. A bend cue must be able to interrupt an image-only obstacle caution, while reliable collision warnings retain priority. Turn **Use normalized phone slope** OFF, then set **Video slope** to ±3°, ±6°, ±10° and ±15° to exercise all four short uphill/downhill cue bands; level for two seconds re-arms the grade event. Video hills alone must not be treated as IMU grade. Turn video test OFF and mount/normalize on level ground for physical vehicle slope tests. See [Camera driving guidance](DRIVING_GUIDANCE.md) for setup and evidence limits.
13. In Android Desk, enable manual gear guidance and simulated engine load, set gear 3, pitch zero and TTI 20. RPM 3500 should request an upshift once; RPM 900 a downshift once; RPM 2000 neither. Automatic mode, neutral/first/top gear, missing/stale RPM and no load must not invent shifts. These are simulated inputs; Camera has conditional gear advice until a vehicle adapter supplies live data.
14. In Camera, enable **Use normalized phone slope** and **Slope guidance**. Fix the forward-facing phone in a stationary angle fixture on level ground and normalize. Verify near-zero pitch regardless of initial mount tilt or landscape direction. Raise/lower the camera's forward direction by known angles: the display should report positive uphill/negative downhill degrees and `100 × tan(angle)` percent grade. Sustained slopes use 2°, 5°, 8° and 12° angle bands, speaking once after 1.5 seconds. A steeper band or changed direction can speak after the six-second guidance gap; returning to an already announced band must stay quiet until a two-second level interval. Manual gear phrases are conditional. Check 0.5° boundary hysteresis without speech below an entry angle. At 5 km/h on a fresh clear straight-road view with steady motion, expect “Maintain speed” instead; show an obstacle and confirm it receives the obstacle caution. Known stationary speed must suppress slope action cues. Check stale data, shock and excessive tilt do not display a valid grade. Lost or unsupported slope readings should report unavailable once after 1.5 seconds, while initial normalization stays quiet. Video speed remains the explicit video setting, while normalization/braking/shock state remains measured. This fixture acceptance test has not yet been run on the phone; it is separate from synthetic core tests.

15. Camera health/recovery: confirm **Vision active: 0 road users** on an empty scene and a nonzero count on a clear person/vehicle. Check that the displayed capture age increases on a slow phone and **Vision delayed** appears beyond 350 ms; slow detection boxes must not disappear immediately upon arrival. Boxes must expire after two seconds without a completed result or three seconds of capture age. No expired result may trigger hazard advice. Thermal rate reduction must be visible; critical heat must show a pause and automatically resume after cooling. Use **Restart detection** to rebind Camera and reload models, including after a model initialization failure; permission errors and camera/model errors must stay visible. Verify background/resume and Camera/MP4/Desk switches do not publish results from an old source. Physical-device failure/recovery validation remains outstanding.

## Latency and thermal acceptance procedure

Measure in a **release build on each physical phone**, with the actual model versions and installed voice recorded. Debug UI timing is not a hardware benchmark.

| Stage | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Frame capture age | Compare capture timestamp in the verified monotonic timebase with analyzer entry |
| Preprocessing | Timestamp around borrowed-plane conversion and tensor fill |
| Models | Measure Supercombo and the separate semantic pass, including provider synchronization |
| Core decision | End inference to audio worker acceptance |
| PCM output | Acceptance to first platform audio frame, including audio route/focus |
| Audible output | Loopback recording or external microphone, accounting for speaker / Bluetooth buffering |

Collect at least 10,000 frame samples after warmup; report p50/p95/p99, maximum, dropped-frame count, peak RSS, battery current and model/provider identity. **The requirement is capture-to-voice-trigger <70 ms; audible onset is a separate measurement.** Reject configurations whose measured p99 exceeds the target. Sustained 30 FPS needs a model loop below 33.3 ms, which is stricter than a 70 ms trigger budget.

Run more than 30 minutes at ordinary and warm ambient conditions. Use the current scheduling limits documented above and inspect the delivered FPS and thermal logs. Any sustained drop below 20 FPS fails the supplied integration criterion; thermal protection must remain enabled. Check resumption, permission revocation, GPS outages, audio focus interruptions, headset routing, app suspension, video EOF and corrupted files.

The UI's vision value measures preprocessing/inference/postprocessing only. It does not prove end-to-end or audible-onset latency. Acceptance remains open until real model/device logs exist.

The supplied phone screenshot shows about 179 ms processing. Rules now allow capture ages below 350 ms and conservatively deduct that age from TTI. Future-dated results and results at or beyond the age cap remain invalid. Tracking uses the same maximum frame interval. These changes allow bounded slower processing; the <70 ms/30 FPS target remains unfulfilled on that screenshot, and phone validation of the new configuration is still required.
