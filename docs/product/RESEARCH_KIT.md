# Instructor and learner research kit

Status: ready-to-use protocol; no participants recruited or interviewed yet.
Participation/recording/export must be voluntary. Record decisions and unresolved
disagreements; do not present engineering assumptions as instructor endorsement.

## Learner session — about 25 minutes

Ask about their last supervised lesson: the difficult moment, what they noticed,
what the instructor said and what helped. Ask when another voice would distract
them. Ask about mount placement, phone model, preferred language and audio route.
Avoid beginning with a feature pitch or asking whether they like AI.

Show short pre-recorded situations and candidate cues while stationary. Ask the
participant to explain the cue, what they would do, whether it arrived early
enough and whether silence would be better. Include correct, unnecessary and
late cues. Do not ask them to perform dangerous encounters on a road.

Finish by asking what they would practise next, whether the review provides a
useful example, what would make them stop using the app and whether they would
choose to use it during another supervised lesson.

## Instructor session — about 40 minutes

Review candidate operating conditions and the first-release capability matrix.
Independently mark the earliest reasonable and latest useful cue time for each
encounter. Distinguish lead, parked, oncoming and roadside road users. Identify
when the driver is already responding correctly and additional speech is unhelpful.

Review phrase meaning, urgency, anxiety/distraction and language. Assess whether
the app implies knowledge it does not have, including road grade, speed limits,
gear/RPM and rear visibility. Mark unsupported claims and required changes.

Use `PHRASE_REVIEW.csv` as the starting worksheet: it contains all 43 current
English phrases, marks every decision pending, and identifies research-only
emergency phrases. Preserve separate original reviews before adjudication.

## Record template

Participant pseudonym; role; experience range; consent/recording choice; phone;
language; scenario ID; cue and time; interpretation; helpful/unnecessary/late/
harmful/uncertain; distraction rating with explanation; suggested wording;
practice takeaway; voluntary reuse response; interviewer notes.

Two reviewers should independently label an overlapping sample. Preserve their
original labels and have an instructor resolve disagreements with reasons.
Keep personal identifiers separate from the research notes and exports.

## Completion evidence

Aim initially for 10–15 learners and 3–5 instructors, then decide whether the
sample covers the intended users. Report counts and disagreements. Produce a
versioned phrase rubric, revised support conditions and explicit open questions.
This small study establishes product direction; it cannot demonstrate crash reduction.
